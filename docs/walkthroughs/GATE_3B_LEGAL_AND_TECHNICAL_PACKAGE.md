### 1. Memorandum for Legal Counsel and Use-of-Force Experts

**From**: Author & Editorial Team, *Time, Distance, and Power*  
**Subject**: Pre-Publication Legal & Civil Liability Audit of Chapters 20 and 21  
**Purpose**: *Time, Distance, and Power* is a non-fiction technical manual instructing civilian martial artists, law enforcement officers, and military personnel on the biomechanics, decision dynamics, and survival realities of violent conflict. 

Chapters 20 and 21 provide definitive tactical instructions on asymmetric threats (edged weapons, multi-opponent swarms, and contact firearm disarms) and statutory self-defense doctrine (objective reasonableness, disparity of force, cessation boundaries, 911 dispatch scripts, and post-incident silence). 

While the manuscript grounds its doctrine in constitutional jurisprudence (*Graham v. Connor*, 1989), emergency medicine (TCCC), and standard police critical incident protocols, self-defense law varies significantly across jurisdictions. This package extracts the five highest-liability tactical doctrines and flags them for counsel review to ensure that our instructional language provides maximum legal protection for both the reader and the publisher.

### 2. Prioritized Legal Review Flags

```
================================================================================
FLAG 1: PREEMPTIVE ACTION, THE REACTIONARY GAP, AND THE 21-FOOT RULE
Location: Section 20.1 (Edged Weapons & Flanking)
================================================================================
```
- **Text Excerpt**:
  > *"Action beats reaction by 200 to 400 milliseconds. If you wait until a concealed knife clears fabric and accelerates into a stabbing arc, you are already behind the temporal curve... If an aggressor with an edged weapon closes inside twenty-one feet, they occupy your reactionary deficit zone. At this range, verbal warnings and defensive posturing fail. You must execute an immediate, explosive tactical choice: break spatial contact by sprinting to cover, or violently collapse distance to control the weapon limb before the blade deploys."*
- **Legal & Liability Implications**:
  1. *Perception vs. Reality*: If a defender initiates explosive physical contact based on pre-attack intent cues (e.g., reaching toward a waistband) before a blade is visually confirmed, prosecutors in strict duty-to-retreat states may argue that the defender initiated an unprovoked battery.
  2. *Tueller Drill Application*: The "21-foot rule" is an empirical police training observation (*Dennis Tueller*, 1983) demonstrating the time required to draw a sidearm against a charging knife threat ($1.5\ \text{s}$ over $6.4\ \text{m}$), not a statutory legal perimeter granting automatic deadly force immunity to civilians.
- **Counsel Guidance Requested**:
  - Should the text include an explicit legal disclaimer clarifying that the 21-foot distance is an engineering reactionary buffer rather than a legal "shoot/strike on sight" zone?
  - Recommended framing for civilian defenders in common-law retreat jurisdictions.

```
================================================================================
FLAG 4: APPLICATION OF GRAHAM V. CONNOR TO PRIVATE CITIZENS & DUTY TO RETREAT
Location: Section 21.1 (Proportionality and the Continuum of Force)
================================================================================
```
- **Text Excerpt**:
  > *"The foundational doctrine governing defensive violence in American jurisprudence is Objective Reasonableness, established by the United States Supreme Court in Graham v. Connor (1989)... The court recognizes that violent encounters are tense, uncertain, and rapidly evolving. Therefore, the law asks: Would a reasonable person, standing in your shoes, facing the identical threat, and operating under survival terror, perceive that level of force as necessary?"*
- **Legal & Liability Implications**:
  1. *4th Amendment Police Standard vs. State Self-Defense Statutes*: *Graham v. Connor*, 490 U.S. 386 (1989), specifically interprets the Fourth Amendment's prohibition against unreasonable seizures by government agents (law enforcement). While many state courts apply an "objective reasonableness" test to civilian self-defense, civilian standards are defined by state statutory codes (e.g., Model Penal Code § 3.04) and common-law jury instructions, which often incorporate duty-to-retreat, initial-aggressor, and proportionality rules not analyzed in Graham.
  2. *Stand Your Ground vs. Duty to Retreat*: The text does not distinguish between the ~38 "Stand Your Ground" states and the ~12 "Duty to Retreat" states, which require a civilian to safely retreat if possible before deploying lethal force.
- **Counsel Guidance Requested**:
  - Counsel should review whether framing civilian self-defense primarily through *Graham v. Connor* inadvertently confuses readers regarding their state's statutory retreat obligations.
  - Recommended addition of a jurisdictional notice clarifying that private citizens must consult local state statutes regarding retreat duties.

```
================================================================================
FLAG 5: THE 24-HOUR RULE, POST-INCIDENT STATEMENTS, AND 5TH AMENDMENT SILENCE
Location: Section 21.2 (Post-Incident Procedures: Medical, Exit, and Legal Preservations)
================================================================================
```
- **Text Excerpt**:
  > *"Internal affairs policies strictly mandate The 24-Hour Rule for officers following critical force incidents. Officers cannot give formal recorded depositions until at least one or two full sleep cycles pass... As a civilian defender, protect yourself through Cognitive Preservation: confirm your identity, state that an assault occurred, point out transient evidence, and state firmly: 'Officer, I was attacked and forced to defend my life. I will cooperate fully, but I am in physical shock, I require medical evaluation, and I request my attorney present before answering detailed questions.' Then maintain absolute silence."*
- **Legal & Liability Implications**:
  1. *Civilian vs. Police Officer Status*: Police officers operate under employment contracts, collective bargaining agreements, and *Garrity v. New Jersey* (1967) protections granting administrative delays before internal affairs statements. A civilian on the street has no statutory right to a 24-hour delay before questioning; while civilians possess absolute Fifth and Sixth Amendment rights, invoking them may result in immediate custodial arrest and booking.
  2. *Pre-Arrest Silence in Court*: In some federal circuits and state courts (e.g., *Salinas v. Texas*, 2013), pre-Miranda silence during non-custodial questioning can be introduced by prosecutors as substantive evidence of guilt unless explicitly invoked under the Fifth Amendment.
- **Counsel Guidance Requested**:
  - Ensure the invocation script explicitly states: *"I am invoking my Fifth Amendment right to remain silent and my Sixth Amendment right to counsel"* to prevent pre-custodial silence from being weaponized.
  - Clarify to the reader that asserting these rights may result in temporary arrest and booking, but protects against catastrophic self-incrimination.
