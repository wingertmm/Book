### 4. Planned Structure & Section Outline (Beat Breakdown)

#### Beat 1: Section 21.2: Post-Incident Procedures: Medical, Exit, and Legal Preservations — The Golden Ten Minutes
- **Lead Tactical Rule**: *The fight is not over when the attacker falls; scan 360 degrees, check your baseline, and secure your immediate perimeter.*
- **The Combat Reality**:
  - The dangerous post-fight psychological lull: the opponent collapses, and the defender drops their hands, exhales, and stares at the ground.
  - The **Golden Ten Minutes**: The immediate aftermath dictates whether you survive secondary threats, bleed to death unnoticed, or spend life in prison.
  - **Immediate Actions on Neutralization**:
    1. *360-Degree Scan*: Breaking tunnel vision to detect incoming accomplices, hostile onlookers, or vehicular threats (recalling Chapter 20 multi-opponent doctrine).
    2. *Weapon Containment*: If carrying a defensive tool, return it immediately to holster or secure retention. Do not stand over a bleeding person holding a drawn weapon when first responders arrive.
    3. *Scene Decision Matrix (Stay vs. Relocate)*:
       - If the environment remains violently hostile (hostile crowd swelling, gang territory, uncontained threat accomplices), perform tactical relocation to the nearest secure, populated sanctuary.
       - If the scene is secure, remain on site. Unjustified flight creates a legal presumption of guilt ("flight from the scene") that prosecutors will weaponize in court.

#### Beat 2: Stress-Induced Analgesia & Physical Self-Triage
- **Lead Tactical Rule**: *Never assume you are uninjured because you feel no pain; adrenaline masks catastrophic trauma.*
- **The Biological Mechanism of Stress-Induced Analgesia**:
  - Under Chapter 18's sympathetic surge, massive Beta-endorphin and dynorphin release shuts down dorsal horn nociceptive signaling in the spinal cord.
  - In edged weapon and close-quarters assaults (Chapter 20), victims routinely state they "never felt the knife." They believed they were merely hit with blunt fists, only discovering multiple puncture wounds when blood fills their shoes or hypovolemic shock causes syncope.
- **The Hands-On Blood Sweep Protocol**:
  - Before making calls or talking to bystanders, execute an aggressive, hands-on self-triage:
    - Strip gloves and run bare palms firmly over the neck, armpits, chest, abdomen, back, groin, and extremities.
    - Visually inspect your palms after each sweep for bright red arterial or dark venous blood.
  - Immediate tactical trauma intervention (tourniquet high and tight, wound packing, occlusive chest seals). Survival always takes precedence over legal paperwork.

#### Beat 3: 911 Call Architecture & First Responder Interaction
- **Lead Tactical Rule**: *Treat the 911 call as an unalterable courtroom transcript; state the facts, request medical aid, describe your appearance, and stop talking.*
- **The 911 Audio Record**:
  - Dispatch recordings are recorded automatically and admitted as unvarnished evidence. Babbling, angry curses, or speculative statements ("I showed that guy!") will be played repeatedly to a jury.
- **The 5-Point 911 Call Architecture**:
  1. *Location*: State exact address or cross-streets first (so aid can dispatch even if the line drops).
  2. *Medical and Police Dispatch*: Request both emergency services immediately.
  3. *Identify as Victim / State Threat Status*: "I was attacked by an individual. The attacker is down and requires medical assistance."
  4. *Suspect Description*: Provide clothing, gender, build, and any known weapons.
  5. *Self-Description & Safe Demeanor*: "I am wearing a navy jacket and jeans. I am unarmed, my hands will be empty, and I will be waiting near the entrance."
- **First Responder Arrival Protocol**:
  - Arriving officers enter an unknown, high-threat scene with firearms unholstered.
  - Empty hands spread open at chest level, fingers wide. Zero sudden gestures, zero pointing, zero running toward police cruisers. Comply with all commands instantly, even if ordered onto the ground.

#### Beat 4: The 24-Hour Rule & Cognitive Preservation
- **Lead Tactical Rule**: *Never give a detailed formal interview during the adrenaline crash; preserve your rights, request counsel, and allow your memory to consolidate.*
- **The Post-Adrenaline Autonomic Crash**:
  - Within 15 to 30 minutes of life-or-death combat, epinephrine and norepinephrine levels crash, precipitating acute physical and cognitive exhaustion.
  - Symptoms: uncontrolled shivering, nausea, severe perceptual distortions (tunnel vision, auditory exclusion, temporal distortion). You cannot accurately recount how many strikes occurred or the exact distance between bodies.
- **The 24-Hour Rule & Law Enforcement Precedent**:
  - Major police agencies explicitly forbid immediate interrogation of involved officers following lethal force incidents. Standard operating procedure mandates 24 to 48 hours and at least one full sleep cycle before giving a sworn deposition, allowing cortisol levels to recede and the hippocampus to consolidate fragmented memory engrams.
- **Cognitive Preservation Protocol**:
  - Civilians must exercise the exact same protective protocol:
    1. Identify yourself and identify witnesses and physical evidence (e.g., "His knife is under that car; those two bystanders saw him charge me").
    2. Deliver the professional legal invocation: *"Officer, I was attacked and forced to defend my life. I want to cooperate fully with your investigation, but I am in physical shock, I need medical evaluation, and I require my attorney present before answering detailed questions or giving a formal statement."*
    3. Invoke your Fifth and Sixth Amendment rights and remain completely silent.
