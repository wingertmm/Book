### 4. Planned Structure & Section Outline (Beat Breakdown)

- **Lead Tactical Rule**: *Never deliberate between competing options under fire; execute the first viable action script recognized by your training.*

- **The RPD Architecture in Combat**: Applying Gary Klein’s Recognition-Primed Decision model. Instead, the brain matches the threat's angle, forward velocity, and posture to trained mental templates, instantly selecting the first *satisficing* (functional) action script. If the threat charges linearly with chin high, the practitioner retrieves a bilateral wedge frame and angle cut without evaluating twenty alternative defenses.

#### Beat 2: Step 4 — Act: Actions on Contact and Breaking Cognitive Stalls
- **Lead Tactical Rule**: *Initiate immediate, default structural movement upon stimulus; violent contact must trigger muscle action before cognitive doubt intervenes.*

- **Battle Drills on the Mat: Actions on Contact**: In military tactics, contact with the enemy triggers immediate, standardized battle drills without waiting for orders. In personal defense, Actions on Contact are pre-drilled gross-motor responses:
  - *The Default High Frame Wedge*: Forearms formed into a rigid skeletal wedge, chin tucked behind shoulder, driving 45 degrees offline.
  - *The Sprawl Frame*: Hips dropped instantly upon downward pelvic pressure or leg contact.
- **Defeating Cognitive Freezing**: Initiating an Action on Contact dissipates incoming impulse ($J = \int F\, dt$), protects vital structures, and seizes the physical initiative, buying the fractions of a second needed for adaptive assessment.

#### Beat 3: Step 4 — Reassess: Closed-Loop Feedback Under Pressure

### 5. Callout Block Design ("The Mechanics Behind It")

> [!NOTE]
> ### The Mechanics Behind It: RPD Retrieval Latency, Actions on Contact Impulse Mechanics, and Closed-Loop Control Latency
>
> 1. **Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) Retrieval Latency & Pattern Complexity**:  
>    In naturalistic decision settings, retrieval latency for a satisficing action schema follows an indexed pattern search:
>    $$t_{\text{RPD}} = t_{\text{match}} + t_{\text{eval}} = \alpha \cdot \log_2(M) + \Delta t_{\text{threshold}}$$
>    Where $M$ is the number of trained prototypical combat templates and $\alpha \approx 35\ \text{ms/bit}$. For an indexed memory store ($M \le 8$ core primitives per priority), retrieval requires:
>    $$t_{\text{RPD}} \approx 35 \cdot \log_2(8) + 15\ \text{ms} \approx 120\ \text{ms}$$
>    In contrast, multi-attribute utility comparison across $n$ candidate moves evaluating $m$ operational attributes scales linearly: $t_{\text{MAUT}} \propto n \cdot m \cdot \beta$, routinely exceeding $750\text{--}900\ \text{ms}$, resulting in fatal cognitive stalls under incoming strikes.
>
> 2. **Actions on Contact Impulse Mechanics & Dynamic Work Redirection**:  
>    An incoming assault delivers linear impulse governed by the momentum change equation:
>    $$\vec{J} = \int_{0}^{\Delta t} \vec{F}_{\text{contact}}(t)\, dt = \Delta \vec{p} = m_{\text{threat}} \Delta \vec{v}$$
>    Deploying an Action on Contact wedge frame converts direct linear collision into angular deflection. By introducing an eccentric contact vector $\vec{r}_{\text{frame}}$ at angle $\theta = 45^\circ$, the normal force $\vec{F}_N$ generates rotational torque:
>    $$\vec{\tau} = \vec{r}_{\text{frame}} \times \vec{F}_N = I \vec{\alpha}$$
>    This deflects the assailant's linear drive vector away from the defender's center of mass, dissipating kinetic energy over an extended displacement ($\Delta x_{\text{frame}}$) and reducing peak cranial deceleration force by over $60\%$.
>
> 3. **Closed-Loop Feedback Latency: Tactile Proprioception vs. Visual Cortical Channels**:  
>    The total latency of a biological closed-loop control system is the sum of sensor transduction, neural transmission/processing, and motor effector delay:
>    $$t_{\text{loop}} = \tau_{\text{sensor}} + \tau_{\text{processing}} + \tau_{\text{effector}}$$
>    For visual feedback channels: $\tau_{\text{retina}} \approx 40\ \text{ms}$, $\tau_{\text{cortical}} \approx 100\ \text{ms}$, and $\tau_{\text{motor}} \approx 60\ \text{ms}$, yielding a minimum visual loop time:
>    $$t_{\text{loop, visual}} \approx 200\text{--}250\ \text{ms}$$
>    For tactile proprioceptive mechanoreceptors (Pacinian and Meissner corpuscles in contact frames): $\tau_{\text{tactile}} \approx 15\ \text{ms}$, spinal/subcortical reflex loop $\tau_{\text{subcortical}} \approx 35\ \text{ms}$, and motor recruitment $\tau_{\text{motor}} \approx 40\ \text{ms}$:
>    $$t_{\text{loop, tactile}} \approx 90\text{--}100\ \text{ms}$$
>    Tactile closed-loop feedback processes adjustments in less than half the time of visual tracking, proving that physical framing and clinch contact provide a vastly superior guidance system under combat pressure.
