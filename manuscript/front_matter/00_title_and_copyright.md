#### 2. Training Tool Safety & Contact Disarm Protocols
Under no circumstances should any reader attempt to practice the weapon retention, knife disarm, or firearm contact manipulation drills (specifically including the slide-clamp, out-of-battery, or rotational torque techniques detailed in Chapter 20) with live firearms, loaded magazines, chambered ammunition, or sharpened edged weapons. 

All tactical weapon drills must be conducted under the direct supervision of certified instructors utilizing dedicated, non-firing inert training tools (such as solid polymer blue guns, inert barrel blocks, or rounded aluminum/rubber training knives) within an inspected, secure training environment. Practicing contact firearm maneuvers with live weapons constitutes extreme negligence and creates lethal danger of accidental discharge.

#### 3. Jurisdictional Self-Defense Law Notice
The legal frameworks discussed in Chapter 21—including the Objective Reasonableness standard established by the United States Supreme Court in *Graham v. Connor* (1989), the Continuum of Force, Disparity of Force, and the 21-foot reactionary buffer—are analytical engineering models and constitutional benchmarks. They do not constitute formal legal advice.

Self-defense statutes, common-law doctrines, Castle Doctrine protections, and Stand-Your-Ground provisions vary significantly across international, federal, state, and municipal jurisdictions. In approximately twelve U.S. states and numerous international jurisdictions, private citizens are subject to a statutory **Duty to Retreat** before deploying lethal force in public spaces. Furthermore, *Graham v. Connor* specifically interprets Fourth Amendment limits on governmental law enforcement seizures; private civilian self-defense is governed by state-specific criminal statutes and common-law jury instructions. Readers are strongly advised to consult qualified legal counsel and review their local state criminal codes regarding the justified use of force in self-defense.

#### 4. Post-Incident Rights & Professional Invocations
The post-incident protocols outlined in Section 21.2 (including the 24-Hour Rule, the 911 Call Architecture, and Cognitive Preservation) describe standard law enforcement critical-incident operating procedures and the exercise of constitutional Fifth and Sixth Amendment rights. While asserting these rights preserves legal integrity, readers must understand that asserting the right to remain silent or requesting legal counsel at the scene of a violent encounter may result in temporary investigative detention or custodial arrest. Always consult an attorney licensed in your jurisdiction for guidance on post-incident criminal defense procedures.
