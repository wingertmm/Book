# Manuscript Style & Voice Guide: Time, Distance, and Power

Authoritative rules for tone, vocabulary, and technical formatting for *Time, Distance, and Power*.

---

## 1. Authorial Voice & Tone: Fighter-First, Engineer-Backed
- **Fighter-First, Engineer-Backed Persona**: Write as an experienced fighter, coach, and Air Force officer who possesses the unfair advantage of a mechanical engineering mind (BS, MS)—**never as an academic lecturing down to martial artists**. The engineering lens is a differentiator only when it directly clarifies and sharpens a fighting decision.
- **The Mat Test**: If an explanation sounds like an engineering dissertation rather than something an experienced black-belt coach would say to a student between rounds on the mat, rewrite it. 
- **Pragmatic & Objective**: Avoid machismo, bravado, and bar-fight glorification. Analyze violence dispassionately as a physical system governed by physical laws and human decision constraints.
- **Accessible Synthesis**: Translate military doctrine and engineering principles into clean, intuitive civilian language. For example, use *"Clear purpose + flexible method"* rather than bureaucratic jargon like *"Commander's Intent"*.
- **Spelling & Branding Standard**: Use standard English spelling: **Fighting** (never "Phighting").

---

## 2. Core Writing Rules & The Hierarchy of Delivery
- **Action Precedes Math (The Inverted Delivery Pyramid)**:
  1. *Lead with the visceral tactical reality and decision rule*: What is happening? What is the risk or consequence?
  2. *Explain the physical mechanism in plain, punchy language*: Focus on base, posture, fulcrums, levers, and balance.
  3. *Offer the formal engineering equation or system label as supporting proof*: Formulas ($\tau = r \times F$, $W = F \times d$) and systems labels (TRIZ, closed-loop damping) must confirm what the reader already visualized, placed in concise parentheticals or dedicated callout sidebars.
- **Concrete Analogies Over Abstract Jargon**:
  - Instead of *"axial loading vs. bending moments"*, write: *"treating your forearm like a steel support beam instead of trying to bench-press a 200-pound man."*
  - Instead of *"Hick's Law decision-tree collapse"*, write: *"offering an irresistible bait so you know exactly which punch they will throw before they throw it."*
  - Instead of *"closed-loop damping under high entropy"*, write: *"riding the opponent's violent thrashing like a shock absorber while staying calm inside the storm."*
- **Principles Over Techniques**: Never write a catalogue of isolated moves (e.g., "10 moves to escape a headlock"). Every physical action mentioned must serve as a concrete illustration of an invariant principle (e.g., maintaining *Security of Position* or *Maneuver to Advantage*).
- **Show, Don't Tell**: Instead of asserting that "timing is critical", contrast the 500-millisecond reaction window of an untrained attacker against the 100-millisecond window of a trained practitioner.
- **Stress-Grounded**: Every tactic discussed must hold up against the physiological realities of adrenaline: tunnel vision, loss of fine motor skills, and auditory exclusion.
- **Active Voice Mandate**: Keep passive voice strictly under 10% across all narrative and instructional sections.

---

## 3. Banned Marketing Fluff & Words
The following words weaken technical credibility and are strictly prohibited in the manuscript:
- ❌ "Phighting"
- ❌ "seamlessly"
- ❌ "natively"
- ❌ "cleanly"
- ❌ "simply" / "just"
- ❌ "obviously"
- ❌ "lethal" / "deadly" (in promotional contexts)
- ❌ "foolproof" / "guaranteed"

---

## 4. Visual & Structural Elements
- **Callout Blocks**:
  - `> [!NOTE]`: **The Mechanics / Doctrine Behind It**: Dedicated sidebars containing formal engineering formulas ($\tau = r \times F$, $W = \int F \, dx$), TRIZ inventive principles, and historical military doctrine. Keeps deep technical context available for analytical readers without cluttering main narrative flow.
  - `> [!IMPORTANT]`: Non-negotiable safety rules or invariant combat principles.
  - `> [!CAUTION]`: High-risk failure modes (e.g., trading position for a low-percentage submission or diving into a double-leg on concrete).
- **Diagrams**: Use Mermaid diagrams for decision flows (e.g., the 4-step decision cycle) and ASCII range charts for space control.
