# Book Glossary & Index: Time, Distance, and Power

Alphabetical definitions of foundational terms, doctrines, and combat principles.

---

### A
- **Actions on Contact**: An immediate, pre-trained tactical sequence executed without hesitation the instant violence begins: Recognize $\rightarrow$ Lock Purpose $\rightarrow$ Choose $\rightarrow$ Act $\rightarrow$ Reassess.
- **Adrenaline-Induced Degradation**: The physiological breakdown of conscious reasoning, peripheral vision (tunnel vision), and fine motor control when heart rates exceed ~145–175 BPM.
- **Angular Flanking**: Stepping or pivoting off the attacker's line of force (centerline) to occupy an oblique or rear angle where their primary weapons cannot bear.
- **Asymmetric Limb Ratio**: The tactical mathematics of limb commitment wherein one defensive limb or structural anchor immobilizes two or more of an opponent's limbs or movement axes (e.g., 1-on-2 or 1-on-3), generating a numerical surplus in uncommitted operational limbs.
- **Asymmetry of Habit Unlearning**: The neurological reality that extinguishing an automated motor error requires active prefrontal inhibition, making unlearning a bad habit vastly more difficult and cognitively expensive than learning a correct skill from the start.
- **Avoidance When Superior**: The highest form of self-defense; resolving a crisis through awareness, de-escalation, or immediate departure without applying physical power.

### B
- **Base of Support (BOS)**: The structural foundation area bounded by an individual's points of contact with the ground; keeping the Center of Mass vertically aligned within this perimeter is required to maintain static and dynamic equilibrium.

### C
- **Centerline**: The imaginary vertical plane passing through the center of an opponent's body where their primary striking and driving mass are concentrated.
- **Clear Purpose + Flexible Method**: The self-defense adaptation of mission command; locking the strategic objective (e.g. exit safely) while retaining decentralized freedom to choose the tactical tool.
- **Close Distance**: The combat range within arm's reach where elbows, knees, and head-control frames dominate.
- **Command Center**: The higher cortical faculties (prefrontal cortex) responsible for strategic intent, threat auditing, cue reading, and choosing when to engage or disengage before contact.
- **Concentration**: Applying overwhelming force at a decisive moment and structural vulnerability rather than scattering weak, piecemeal strikes.

### D
- **Decision-Cycle Dominance**: Moving through the recognition-to-action sequence faster than an attacker, forcing them into a perpetually lagging reactive state.
- **Degrees of Freedom (DOF)**: The independent spatial and rotational parameters (three translational, three rotational) defining the movement capability of a physical body; pinning systems operate by systematically constraining these axes to zero.
- **Distance**: The spatial dimension of combat governing reaction time and weapon eligibility across four distinct ranges.
- **Dual-Brain Combat Architecture**: The operational partition between the conscious Command Center (cortex) and the involuntary motor reflex engine (lizard brain).

### E
- **Economy of Force**: Deploying the minimum necessary effort on secondary distractions so maximum energy and capability remain reserved for the primary objective or exit.

### F
- **Framing (Structural Skeletal Frame)**: The mechanical positioning of bones (forearms, shins, locked limb angles) as rigid structural struts or trusses to absorb, bear, or redirect an opponent's weight and incoming force directly into the ground or pelvis, eliminating metabolic muscle fatigue (Economy of Force) and preventing spatial collapse.

### H
- **Hard Target**: A defender whose posture, structural alignment, situational awareness, and foundational positioning make them difficult and costly to attack.

### I
- **Initiative**: Driving the tempo and direction of the encounter so the attacker is forced to react to you, rather than vice versa.
- **Intent Cues**: Observable physical and bio-mechanical telegraphs (weight transfer, gaze direction, respiratory catch, centerline rotation) that reveal an opponent's commitment prior to motion.
- **Iron Law of Combat Mastery**: *"Thinking without repetition produces hesitation; repetition without thinking produces rigid, unadaptive patterns."*

### J
- **Jamming (Distance Collapse)**: Aggressively closing spatial separation inside an opponent's optimal kinetic or rotational radius (e.g., inside a bat swing arc or straight punch extension) to neutralize leverage ($\tau = r \times F$) and bio-mechanically disable long-range strikes.

### K
- **Kinematic Chain**: The interconnected mechanical assembly of rigid skeletal links (bones) and articulating joints (fulcrums) that transfer energy, acceleration, and force from the ground through the core to the target.

### L
- **Law of Permanence**: The training principle that repetition cements motor patterns regardless of their technical correctness (*"Practice does not make perfect; practice makes permanent. Only perfect practice makes perfect"*), mandating deliberate quality control over mindless volume.
- **Law of Weapon Eligibility**: The bio-mechanical principle stating that physical distance dictates which attacks are possible, instantly pruning the threat matrix and reducing cognitive reaction load under stress.
- **Lizard Brain Offloading**: The neurological process of drilling mechanical fundamentals until execution is managed entirely by lower brain centers as an involuntary reflex, freeing the conscious mind for high-level tactical reading.
- **Long Distance**: The combat range outside physical contact where kicks, projectiles, and early avoidance/exit options exist.

### M
- **Maneuver to Advantage**: Using angular movement, changes in level, or environmental features to degrade an attacker's options while enhancing your own.
- **Medium Distance**: The striking range defined by extended fist reach where two-way trades carry maximum risk.
- **Multifunctional Control (TRIZ Principle 6)**: The application of Altshuller's engineering principle of universality to combat geometry—designing holds, pins, or frames where a single defensive limb or wedge accomplishes multiple simultaneous control functions (e.g., a leg pinning an arm, restricting hip elevation, and misaligning the spine).

### N
- **Near Distance**: Complete body-to-body engagement involving grips, underhooks, clinch fighting, throws, and ground grappling.
- **Neural Conditioning**: The deliberate process of deep physical repetition that encodes combat decisions into subconscious neural pathways, bypassing conscious deliberation under adrenaline.

### O
- **Outcome Focus**: Directing attentional focus toward external effects (e.g., driving through a target, creating 3 feet of separation) rather than internal body mechanics, maximizing strike velocity and impact force under stress (Halperin & Wulf 2016).

### P
- **Power**: The focused application of kinetic force, leverage, and momentum guided strictly by strategic purpose and legal proportionality.
- **Purpose First**: The master rule requiring the primary objective to be decided before or at the moment of contact, judging all tactical choices by whether they advance that goal.

### S
- **Security of Position**: The principle that a defender must never cede a sound physical, structural, or spatial position for a low-percentage strike or high-risk exchange.
- **Setups & Off-Balancing**: Creating mechanical or cognitive vulnerabilities in an opponent (via misdirection, level changes, or structural breaks) before launching a decisive action.
- **Spatial Trade-Off (Zero-Distance Paradox)**: The tactical dilemma wherein eliminating space (zero distance) achieves maximum physical control and nullifies an opponent's kinetic work, but completely immobilizes the controller; conversely, generating space to maneuver or advance position creates the exact window an opponent requires to escape.
- **Surprise**: Acting outside the attacker's expectations in timing, angle, or method.

### T
- **Time**: The temporal dimension of combat; the reactionary gap and decision window required to observe, orient, decide, and act.
- **Transition Gap (The Escape Window)**: The temporary, dynamic micro-space that inevitably opens when shifting between pinning positions or maneuvering to disengage, representing the primary vulnerability interval where an opponent can frame, shrimp, or reverse.
- **TRIZ (Theory of Inventive Problem Solving)**: The engineering heuristic system developed by Genrich Altshuller, applied in combat strategy to resolve physical contradictions and optimize resource efficiency (specifically Principle 6: Universality/Multifunctionality).
